Executive summary

The decision, on one page.

Econet does not need MVNOs to grow subscribers — it needs a strategy for the moment competition is put on the agenda. This proposal is that strategy: lead the opening of the market, on Econet's commercial terms, through a neutral enabler that carries the cost and the risk.

Situation
Econet holds a commanding position — ~72% of subscribers and ~89% of voice traffic (POTRAZ, Q4 2024) — and, as a licensed MNO, already holds the right to host MVNOs. But raw wholesale margins are thin, and a dominant operator is the natural focus of a regulator's drive for competition. Pressure to "level the playing field" is a matter of when, not if.
Tension
If Econet waits, the market is likely opened for it — through mandated, price-regulated wholesale access on terms Econet does not control. Doing it in-house instead carries an inherent conflict of interest that a regulator will not credit as genuine competition.
Recommendation
Move first. Launch a voluntary, commercially-structured MVNO programme now, enabled by a neutral third-party MVNE. Econet keeps control of pricing and partner selection, converts thin wholesale into wholesale-plus-enablement margin, and presents the regulator a market that is already competitive — defusing the pressure before it hardens into regulation.
The ask
Endorse the strategic direction, mandate a joint commercial & wholesale framework, approve the neutral-enabler model, and green-light a time-boxed pilot with the first anchor MVNOs — targeting soft launch in ~6 months, at zero capex to Econet.
Defend

Every brand on Econet's network is share that does not move to NetOne or Telecel.

Monetise

Turn spare and off-peak capacity into a new, high-margin annuity — at near-zero incremental cost.

De-risk

Get ahead of the regulator and shape the wholesale framework rather than inherit it.

Stay focused

The enabler carries operations, compliance and CX. Exco bandwidth stays on network, 5G and EcoCash.

Section 02 · The decision

Two ways this market opens. Only one of them is on your terms.

MVNOs will arrive in Zimbabwe. The strategic variable Econet still controls is how — reactively, under a regulatory mandate, or proactively, as a commercial choice.

Path A · Wait and react

The market is opened for you.

  • Regulator concludes a market study and mandates wholesale / MVNO access.
  • Wholesale prices set — or capped — by the regulator, not by Econet.
  • Access on non-discriminatory terms to partners you did not choose.
  • Margin compression with no offsetting enablement upside.
  • A public narrative of an operator forced to open up.
Path B · Lead the shift

You open it — and capture the value.

  • A voluntary, commercially-structured MVNO programme — launched now.
  • Wholesale rate card and revenue share set by Econet, commercially.
  • You choose the anchor partners and the segments they serve.
  • Wholesale plus a share of enablement and VAS economics.
  • A market you can show the regulator is already competitive.

Path B does not avoid competition — it means competition happens on terms Econet designs, with a partner that carries the cost, while the regulatory case for intervention weakens because the market is already opening.

Section 03 · The core question

Econet already holds the MVNE right. Here is why partnering beats building.

As a licensed MNO, Econet could enable MVNOs in-house. But an MVNE is a different business with a different cost base, a different capability set — and, critically, a conflict of interest that an in-house team cannot escape. Seven reasons a neutral third-party enabler is the stronger play.

01The decisive one

Regulatory neutrality

A regulator wants to see arm's-length, non-discriminatory wholesale. An in-house MVNE — inside the dominant operator — is exactly the conflict they distrust. A neutral enabler is credible evidence the market is genuinely opening.

02

No channel conflict

An in-house team will, consciously or not, starve MVNOs that compete with Econet's own retail. A neutral enabler removes the conflict — so partners actually commit, and the programme actually scales.

03

Speed & zero capex

The multi-tenant platform already exists. First launch in months, on an OPEX / revenue-share basis. Building the same BSS/OCS in-house is an 18–24 month programme with capex and a team Econet would have to hire.

04

A cost base built for thin margins

MVNOs are lower-ARPU and higher-touch at the margin. A lean, shared, multi-tenant cost base makes thin wholesale profitable — Econet's premium cost-to-serve does not.

05

Risk transfer

SIM registration, KYC, regulatory reporting, revenue assurance, bad debt and customer care all sit with the enabler. Econet's exposure stays cleanly at the wholesale layer.

06

Demand aggregation & pipeline

The enabler brings the brands and manages them as a portfolio — so Econet deals with one wholesale counterparty, not a dozen small licensees each negotiating separately.

07

Executive focus

Running a portfolio of small brands' billing, care and marketing is a distraction from Econet's core agenda. Partnering keeps exco attention on network, 5G and the EcoCash ecosystem.

In one line

Holding the licence answers "can we?". A neutral MVNE answers "should we — credibly, profitably, and without conflict?"

Section 06 · The economics

Turning thin wholesale into a real margin.

The reason wholesale alone disappoints is that it sells only connectivity. The enabler adds brand value and value-added services on top — so Econet earns wholesale and a share of the enablement economics, on the same network, at near-zero incremental cost.

Wholesale only

What the market offers today

  • Revenue = SIM access + metered voice, SMS and data.
  • Priced at the floor, eroding as competition arrives.
  • No participation in the value the brand builds on top.
  • Under a regulated mandate, the price is not even Econet's to set.
Wholesale + enablement

What the MVNE model unlocks

  • Wholesale access plus a share of VAS, fintech, security & content revenue.
  • Minimum-commitment floors that underwrite a predictable baseline.
  • Higher effective yield per SIM as ARPU rises with the value layer.
  • Commercial terms set by Econet — not by a regulator.

Decide on the strategy, not the line item.

The direct P&L is a high-margin, zero-capex annuity — real, but modest against Econet's revenue base. Section 08 · The regulatory play

Get ahead of the regulator — and take the pressure off the table.

The strongest answer to a competition concern is a market that is already competitive. A voluntary, neutral-enabler MVNO programme lets Econet demonstrate exactly that — and shape the framework rather than inherit it.

Pre-empt

Weaken the case for intervention

A market visibly opening on commercial terms undercuts the rationale for mandated, price-regulated access. Good faith, demonstrated — not compelled.

Shape

Author the wholesale framework

Moving first means Econet sets the reference terms and precedent — rather than negotiating from behind a regulatory finding.

Comply

The enabler carries the load

SIM registration, KYC, licensing filings, QoS and reporting are delivered through the MVNE platform — reducing Econet's compliance surface, not adding to it.

The neutrality point, again — because it is the one that matters most

An arm's-length enabler is what makes the opening credible to POTRAZ.

An in-house MVNE inside a ~72%-share operator invites the question, "is this genuine access, or a controlled gate?" A neutral third-party enabler answers it — and turns a defensive posture into a demonstrable commitment to a more competitive market.

Section 09 · Risk & mitigation

The questions exco will ask — answered.

Every material objection to an MVNO programme has a structural mitigation built into the model.

Cannibalisation of Econet's base

MVNOs are steered to segments Econet does not serve well under its own brand — affinity, niche, diaspora, low-end. The base is largely net-new or defended, not transferred.

Channel conflict

A neutral enabler — not Econet's retail team — runs the MVNOs, removing the incentive to starve them and keeping the arrangement clean for the regulator.

Brand dilution

MVNOs run under their own or white-label brands, governed by clear brand and quality standards. Econet's brand is not exposed to partners' positioning.

Data, security & the EcoCash ecosystem

The enabler operates under Econet's data-governance and security standards and Zimbabwe's data-protection law. Sensitive ecosystem assets stay ring-fenced under Econet control.

Wholesale margin erosion

Minimum-commitment floors and an enablement revenue share protect the economics — and beat the alternative of a regulator-set wholesale price.

Dependency on the enabler

Econet retains the licence, the network and the customer numbering. The enabler is a contracted, replaceable operator — capability without loss of control.
